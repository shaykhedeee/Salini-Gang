Research Questions02 / 08
📋 Framework
Three Sub-Questions

Each sub-question maps to a testable hypothesis; results are reported with OLS coefficients and significance levels.

01
📈
Sub-Q 1 — Subsidies

Does subsidy growth significantly drive fiscal deficit growth? Food, fuel, and fertiliser subsidies form the largest discretionary expenditure category.

H₁ Hypothesis

Subsidy acceleration → direct fiscal deterioration. No offsetting revenue effect. Positive β expected.

Mechanism: Food/fuel subsidies expand total expenditure without a corresponding revenue increase, directly widening the deficit in the year of disbursement.
β = +0.550 · p = 0.0002 ***
Statistical Power
02
🏗
Sub-Q 2 — Capital Expenditure

Is capex growth fiscally self-financing via the GDP multiplier? Does productive investment generate enough tax revenue to offset its direct fiscal cost?

H₁ Hypothesis

Capex growth → GDP expansion → higher tax revenues → net fiscal improvement. Negative β expected.

Mechanism: Infrastructure investment generates second-round economic activity, broadening the tax base and producing revenue inflows that exceed the initial expenditure outlay.
β = −0.436 · p = 0.050 *
Statistical Power
03
🔄
Sub-Q 3 — Debt Spiral

Are interest payments an independent cause or a lagged consequence of the fiscal deficit? Do they create a self-reinforcing spiral, or are they endogenous?

H₁ Hypothesis (Endogeneity)

Interest is endogenous: GFD → borrowing → interest payments. Loses significance in full model; positive β in reverse.

Mechanism: Interest obligations are the lagged invoice of past deficit-financed borrowing — the transmission channel, not the origin, of the debt spiral.
Reverse: β = +0.062 · p = 0.047 *
Statistical Power
⚠️
Note — Secondary Question Dropped

The hypothesis that fiscal deficit independently affects interest rates was found statistically insignificant in full-model estimation. Analysis re-centred on the primary expenditure-composition question.

Econometric Approach04 / 08
⚙️ Specification
Log-Ratio OLS Regression

Three justifications for the log-ratio transformation; two models estimated to separate causality from endogeneity.

1

Stationarity: Removes the deterministic upward trend in absolute fiscal series, reducing spurious regression risk.

2

Scale Normalisation: Makes variables comparable across years independent of nominal GDP growth — ₹ crore figures become dimensionless.

3

Elasticity Interpretation: Coefficients represent % change in GFD growth per 1% change in each driver's growth rate.

4

Two Models: Model 1 regresses GFD on all drivers. Model 2 reverses the regression to test whether GFD predicts interest payments — the debt-spiral causality test.

Model 1 — GFD Drivers
Gross Fiscal Deficit ← 5 Expenditure/Revenue Drivers
ln(GFDt/GFDt−1) = β₀
  − β₁·ln(TaxRevt/TaxRevt−1)
  + β₂·ln(Interestt/Interestt−1)
  + β₃·ln(Subsidiest/Subsidiest−1)
  − β₄·ln(CapExt/CapExt−1)
  + β₅·ln(Defencet/Defencet−1) + εt
Model 2 — Reverse Regression (Endogeneity Test)
Interest Payment Growth ← Fiscal Deficit Growth
ln(Interestt/Interestt−1) = β₀ + β₁·ln(GFDt/GFDt−1) + εt

Key Endogeneity Finding

Interest payments are individually significant (p = 0.047) but lose all significance in the full model (p = 0.143). They are endogenous — the lagged invoice of past expenditure decisions, not an independent driver.
Debt management alone cannot fix an expenditure-driven deficit.

Key Findings07 / 08
🏆 Empirical Results
Four Robust Empirical Findings

Twenty years of Central Government data yield four clear, policy-relevant conclusions from OLS regression.

01
📈
Subsidies are the Primary Expenditure Driver

Most statistically robust driver across all specifications. Each 1% acceleration in subsidy growth produces ≈ 0.55% deterioration in fiscal deficit growth.

β = +0.550 · p = 0.0002 *** · 0.1% level
02
💰
Tax Revenue is the Dominant Stabiliser

Greater-than-proportionate effect (|β| > 1). Reflects India's improving compliance and direct tax base expansion over the study period.

β = −1.647 · p = 0.012 ** · 5% level
03
🏗
Capital Expenditure is Self-Correcting

Validates the GDP multiplier hypothesis. Productive investment generates tax revenue that offsets its direct cost — post-COVID capex expansion is empirically justified.

β = −0.436 · p = 0.050 * · Self-financing
04
🔗
Interest Payments are Endogenous — Not Causal

Significant in isolation (p = 0.047) but insignificant in the full model (p = 0.143). They are the lagged invoice of past expenditure decisions — the transmission, not the origin.

Full: p = 0.143 (n.s.) · Reverse: β = +0.062, p = 0.047

Three Policy Levers
🎯
Priority 1 — Subsidy Rationalisation
Post-2014 DBT shift is the right direction. Continued subsidy rationalisation is India's most direct path to structural fiscal balance (β = +0.550).
📈
Priority 2 — Protect Capital Expenditure
Empirically self-financing (β = −0.436). Fiscal consolidation must not be achieved by cutting productive investment; the post-COVID infrastructure push is justified.
🏛
Priority 3 — FRBM & Revenue Broadening
Largest stabiliser magnitude (β = −1.647). Broadening India's tax base and improving GST compliance is essential for long-run structural balance.
