Econometric Approach04 / 08
⚙️ Specification
Log-Ratio OLS Regression

Transformation serves three econometric purposes; two models are estimated to isolate causality from endogeneity.

1

Stationarity: Removes the upward trend in absolute fiscal series, reducing risk of spurious regression in non-stationary time series data.

2

Scale Normalisation: Makes variables comparable across years regardless of nominal GDP growth — ₹ crore figures are rendered dimensionless.

3

Interpretability: Coefficients represent elasticities — % change in GFD growth per 1% change in each driver's growth rate.

4

Two Models: Model 1 regresses GFD on all drivers; Model 2 reverses causality — regressing Interest Payments on GFD to test for the debt spiral.

Key Endogeneity Finding

Interest payments are individually significant in simple regression (p = 0.047) but lose all significance in the full model (p = 0.143). They are endogenous — the lagged invoice of past borrowing, not an independent driver. Debt management alone cannot fix an expenditure-driven deficit.

Summary of Results
Key Findings07 / 08
🏆 Empirical Results
Four Robust Empirical Findings

Twenty years of Central Government data yield four clear, policy-relevant conclusions.

01
📈
Subsidies are the Primary Expenditure Driver

The single most statistically significant and robust driver of fiscal deficit growth. Each percentage point acceleration in subsidy spending produces a near-equivalent deterioration in the deficit.

β = +0.550 · p = 0.0002 *** · Sig. at 0.1%
02
💰
Tax Revenue is the Primary Stabilising Force

Greater-than-proportionate negative effect on deficit growth (elasticity > 1). Reflects India's improving tax compliance and the broadening of the direct tax base over the study period.

β = −1.647 · p = 0.012 ** · Sig. at 5%
03
🏗
Capital Expenditure is Dynamically Self-Correcting

Associated with fiscal improvement, validating the GDP multiplier hypothesis. Productive investment generates tax revenue that offsets its direct cost — post-COVID capex expansion is empirically justified.

β = −0.436 · p = 0.050 * · Self-financing validated
04
🔗
Interest Payments are Endogenous — Not Causal

Significant in isolation but lose all power in the full model. They are the transmission mechanism of the debt spiral — the lagged invoice of past expenditure decisions — not the originating cause.

Full model: p = 0.143 (n.s.) · Reverse: β = +0.062, p = 0.047

Policy Implications
🎯
Priority 1 — Subsidy Rationalisation
The post-2014 shift to Direct Benefit Transfers (DBT) represents steps in the right direction. Continuation is India's most credible path to structural fiscal balance.
📈
Priority 2 — Protect Capital Expenditure
Empirically confirmed as dynamically self-financing (β = −0.436). The post-COVID infrastructure push is consistent with sustainable fiscal management.
🏛
Priority 3 — FRBM & Revenue Broadening
Second most powerful stabiliser (β = −1.647). Broadening India's tax base and improving compliance is essential for long-run structural balance.
